# Stew Registers — Italian, French, Anglo/German

The same hearty-stew chassis (aromatics + legume/meat + liquid + long cook) reads as three different cuisines depending on the register. Introspecting on the lentil-stew work (french-potato-lentils, milk-stout-cottage-lentils, milk-stout-braised-beef), the three registers break down like this:

## Italian / Mediterranean — less is more

- **Tomato only** as the backbone.
- **Earthy vegetables**: beans, green beans.
- **Parmesan cheese.**
- The discipline is restraint — the tomato carries it, and additions crowd it. (Same philosophy as marcella-tomato-sauce's three ingredients, minestrone's committed tomato path, fasolakia-lathera.)

## French — more is more

- **Lots of vegetables still** (the soffrito stays and grows).
- **Replace the tomato backbone with stock.**
- **Mustard and/or cream, and wine** — the french balancer kit, doing silent heavy lifting (starch from lentils/potatoes absorbs them; you shouldn't taste them individually).
- Worked example: french-potato-lentils — italian skeleton, tomato demoted, potato sweetness + silent mustard/cream + mushroom-tamed tomato doing the work the tomato used to do.

## Anglo / German — balancing bitterness

- **Bitterness becomes the thing to balance**: stout or marmite (one, not both — same bitterness budget).
- **Beef flavor becomes primary over vegetables** — the vegetable base shrinks (onions + garlic can be enough).
- **More peas, turnips.**
- **Vinegar and pickles appear** — the sour-into-the-braise move (german Linsensuppe's vinegar finish, sauerbraten logic, pickle juice + diced pickles).
- Worked examples: milk-stout-cottage-lentils (dark build), milk-stout-braised-beef.

## Cross-register rules

- **Balancer kits are register-locked**: french = wine/cream/mustard; anglo/german = stout/pickle. Mixing kits (cream into a stout base, pickle juice into a wine base) is the likely failure mode. (Open edge: mustard's clash is only confirmed against marmite; flemish carbonnade suggests mustard + beer may coexist.)
- **Tomato paste calibrates by register, not color**: the french version needs LESS paste (its ingredients mesh with tomato) even though the anglo version looks darker (mushroom + beer block the red while the bitter base demands more paste to hold the warmth leg).
- **Pureed mushrooms tame the tomato in both non-italian registers** — they let tomato contribute depth without running the show.
